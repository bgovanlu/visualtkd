How to read a diagram

Every form page uses the same conventions. Learn them once and every diagram on the site, and every print sheet, reads the same way.

The floor line

Each form is performed along a fixed line, drawn as it appears in the Kukkiwon books. The Taegeuk forms use 王: three bars joined by a centre line. The black-belt forms have their own shapes: 士 for Koryo, 山 for Keumgang, 工 for Taebaek.

You start at na (the top of the page) facing ga (the bottom). The bars are ra on the left of the page and da on the right, numbered 1–3 from the top. A left turn from the start faces da.

Which way the figures face

Figures on the left and right bars are drawn from the side, facing the way they move. Figures on the centre line face you when moving toward ga and show their back when returning toward na.

Near limbs are white, far limbs are grey. That is how you tell the left arm or leg from the right. Horse- and crane-stance figures face the way the body faces, with the technique out to the side.

Footprints

The circle beside each figure is a top-down view of the feet, turned to the direction you face. The red foot leads (for crane stance, it is the standing foot), and the blue tick on the rim is the direction you face.

The shapes follow the stance: ap seogi short and almost in line, ap kubi long and a fist wide with the rear foot turned out, dwit kubi an L with the heels in line.

Stance chips

Under each figure, a chip names the stance and the leading foot: L · Ap kubi is a left front stance. Back stance is named by its front foot too, so L · Dwit kubi means the left foot is forward.

The chip style tells you the stance at a glance, even from across the room. On the form pages, chips and technique names link to the technique library.

Move numbers

Numbering follows the Kukkiwon count. When one step carries several techniques, each gets a sub-number, so every figure is exactly one count.

Punches are marked bandae when the punching hand matches the front foot, and baro when it is the opposite hand.

On your phone

Phones show the form move by move: swipe or press Play. The small map in the corner of each card shows where you stand on the floor line and the path so far. Switch to Floor plan to see the whole form at once.

On a desktop, click any figure or table row to study it, and use the arrow keys to step through.

Print sheets

Every form has a two-page print sheet (the Print sheet button on its page). The QR code at the top right of each page opens the form on your phone.

References are printed as short links like visualtkd.com/go/kukkiwon-t1. If a video or page moves, the link is updated on the site, so old printouts keep working.

World Taekwondo form names, sequences and counts follow Kukkiwon; ITF patterns follow the ITF Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do. Descriptions are our own words; see each form’s official references for the source text.
