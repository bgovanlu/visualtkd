Naranhi seogi 나란히서기

Parallel stance

ITF Narani junbi sogi 나란히 준비서기 · Parallel ready stance

Feet parallel, one foot-width apart, legs straight and weight even. The stance for gibon junbi at the start and end of a form.

  • Toes point straight ahead
  • Knees soft, not locked

Ap seogi 앞서기

Walking stance

One natural walking step. Both legs straight, weight even, the feet almost in line with each other.

  • Step length about one stride
  • Rear foot may turn out slightly
  • Hips and shoulders face forward

Ap kubi 앞굽이

Front stance

ITF Gunnun sogi 걷는서기 · Walking stance

A long, low stance. About one and a half steps long and one fist wide, the front knee bent over the toes and the rear leg straight.

  • Front shin near vertical, knee over the ball of the foot
  • Rear leg straight, heel down, foot turned out about 30°
  • Most of the weight on the front leg

Dwit kubi 뒷굽이

Back stance

ITF Niunja sogi 니은자서기 · L-stance

Heels in line in an L shape: the front foot points forward, the rear foot turns out 90°. Both knees bend and most of the weight sits on the rear leg. Our chips and instructions name it by the front foot: "L · Dwit kubi" is the left foot forward.

  • Rear knee bent and pushed out over the rear toes
  • Hips and chest turn to the side, not square to the front foot
  • Front foot light, ready to kick

Beom seogi 범서기

Tiger stance

ITF Dwitbal sogi 뒷발서기 · Rear foot stance

A short stance with almost all the weight on the bent rear leg. The front foot rests on its ball, heel raised, about one foot-length ahead.

  • Front knee bent and turned slightly in
  • Body upright, sitting on the rear leg

Nieunja seogi ㄴ자서기

ㄴ-shaped stance

The feet stand at right angles to each other, like the letter ㄴ, heels about one foot-length apart, knees slightly bent and the weight spread evenly. A short, mobile stance between steps.

  • Front foot points forward, rear foot turned 90°
  • Shorter than a back stance; weight on both legs
Chip on the diagrams
Appears in 1 form · 1 move
  • Cheonkwon20-1

Gyeotdari seogi 곁다리서기

Assisting stance

The front knee bends over the front foot while the rear foot comes in close behind and beside the front heel, only its ball on the floor, the rear knee bent in behind the front knee as if supporting it.

  • Rear knee tucked in behind the front knee
  • Weight mostly on the front leg
Chip on the diagrams
Appears in 1 form · 2 moves

Ogeum seogi 오금서기

Reverse crane stance

Like crane stance, standing on one bent leg, but the instep of the lifted foot presses into the back of the standing knee instead of its side.

  • Standing knee bent, weight centred over the foot
  • Lifted foot hooked behind the knee

Gojung sogi 고정서기

Fixed stance

An ITF stance, first met in Won-Hyo. Shaped like the L-stance but a little longer, with the weight shared evenly between the legs and the body half-facing. Named by the front foot: a left fixed stance has the left foot forward. Drawn here with the L-stance figure.

  • Both knees bent, weight even
  • Front foot points forward, rear foot turned out

Soojik sogi 수직서기

Vertical stance

An ITF stance, first met in Hwa-Rang. A short L shape, about one shoulder width from heel to heel, with both legs straight and a little more of the weight on the rear leg. Named by the rear foot, like the L-stance: a left vertical stance has the left foot back. Drawn here with the L-stance figure.

  • Both legs straight
  • Front foot points forward, rear foot turned out about 90°
  • Slightly more weight on the rear leg

Waebal sogi 외발서기

One-leg stance

An ITF stance, first met in Po-Eun. Stand on one leg, the knee slightly bent, with the other foot drawn up beside the standing knee joint, ready to kick. Named by the standing leg. Drawn here with the crane stance figure.

  • Standing knee slightly bent
  • Lifted foot beside the standing knee joint

Low stance

  • Longer than the walking stance by about a foot
  • Front knee well bent, rear leg straight

Juchum seogi 주춤서기

Horse stance

ITF Annun sogi 앉은서기 · Sitting stance

Feet parallel and about two foot-lengths apart, knees bent and pushed outward, weight even, back upright.

  • Knees over the toes, not collapsing inward
  • Back straight, weight even on both feet
