Background

History 역사

How a handful of schools opened in Seoul after 1945 became one martial art with two main branches, and why the forms on this site come in two sets: the World Taekwondo poomsae and the ITF patterns.

Roots, before 1945

Korea has a long record of fighting without weapons. Medieval chronicles describe subak, a bare-hand combat art practised by soldiers and ordinary people alike. Later came kwonbeop, a military fist art written into army manuals after the invasions of the late 1500s, and taekkyeon, a folk game of kicks and sweeps.

During the Japanese occupation (1910–1945), judo and kendo were taught in Korea, and Koreans who studied in Japan or lived in Manchuria learned karate and Chinese boxing. Those arts shaped the first post-war schools far more directly than the older traditions did; the Korean kicking style came back into them over the following decades.

The kwans, 1945–1955

After liberation, a handful of schools (kwan, 관) opened in and around Seoul. The five usually counted as the originals are Chung Do Kwan, Moo Duk Kwan, Song Moo Kwan, Chang Moo Kwan and Ji Do Kwan (first called Yun Moo Kwan). In the army, General Choi Hong Hi and Nam Tae Hi founded Oh Do Kwan in 1954.

Each kwan taught under its own name (tang soo do, kong soo do, kwon bup) and mostly used karate kata as its forms. Over the years they added the higher, faster kicks that came to set the Korean art apart.

One name: taekwondo

11 April 1955A naming board of masters, historians and public figures chose taekwondo (태권도), the name General Choi submitted: tae foot, kwon fist, do way. Not every kwan used it at once.

1959 – 1965The Korea Taekwondo Association (KTA) was formed in 1959 with Choi as its first president. After a few years under another name, it settled as the single national body in the mid-1960s. Promotion tests were unified under it, and sparring became an event at the National Sports Festival in 1963.

The ITF and the Chang Hon patterns

22 March 1966General Choi founded the International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF) in Seoul, with member associations in nine countries, to spread the art abroad.

He also built his own syllabus: the Chang Hon patterns (tul, 틀), named after his pen name. Each pattern is named after a person or event from Korean history, and its move count or diagram often carries a meaning (Yul-Gok has 38 moves for the 38th parallel, where the scholar Yi I was born). The set grew to 24 patterns, one for each hour of the day, and was fixed in his 15-volume Encyclopedia of Taekwon-Do (1983). In the same years ITF technique settled on the rising-and-falling “sine wave” motion that marks the style today.

1972After falling out with the South Korean government, Choi moved to Canada and took the ITF headquarters to Toronto; it later moved to Vienna. His work with North Korea from 1980 onward widened the gap with Seoul.

The Kukkiwon and the WTF

1971 – 1973In Seoul, the KTA under Kim Un-yong built a central training hall. It opened on 30 November 1972 and was named the Kukkiwon (국기원, “house of the national martial art”) in February 1973. From December 1973, dan certificates were issued in the Kukkiwon president's name.

The association also set new forms for the whole country: the eight Taegeuk poomsae for coloured belts and the black-belt poomsae from Koryo to Ilyeo. These replaced the earlier Palgwe forms and the karate kata the kwans had used. The kwans themselves were formally merged in 1978.

28 May 1973Three days after the first World Taekwondo Championships opened at the Kukkiwon, delegates founded the World Taekwondo Federation (WTF) there, with Kim Un-yong as president.

The two bodies split the work. The Kukkiwon is the World Taekwondo Headquarters: it keeps the curriculum and the poomsae, trains instructors and issues dan and poom certificates worldwide. The WTF is the sport's international federation: competition rules, championships and the Olympics. That is why the same style is called “Kukkiwon taekwondo”, “WT style” or “Olympic style”: the poomsae come from the Kukkiwon, the competition from the WTF.

From WTF to World Taekwondo

1988 – 2000Taekwondo was a demonstration sport at the Seoul 1988 and Barcelona 1992 Olympics and became a full medal sport at Sydney 2000, under WTF rules. Poomsae has its own world championships, judged under World Taekwondo's poomsae rules.

23 June 2017At its General Assembly in Muju, the WTF renamed itself World Taekwondo (WT), mainly because “WTF” had taken on an unwanted meaning online. It is the same body; only the name changed. The Kukkiwon is still the headquarters for the art, and its poomsae are the forms on this site's World Taekwondo section.

The ITF after General Choi

General Choi died in Pyongyang in 2002. A dispute over his succession, which had begun the year before, left several federations each calling itself the ITF. The two largest today are the ITF based in Vienna, which publishes the Encyclopedia, and the ITF led by his son Choi Jung Hwa.

All of them teach the Chang Hon patterns, with small differences. The best known is pattern 15: the original Ko-Dang was replaced by Juche in the 1980s, and federations still differ on which name, and which sequence, they use.

Since 2014 the Vienna ITF and World Taekwondo have signed an agreement to cooperate and have given joint demonstrations.

Other branches

Some masters went their own way. The American Taekwondo Association (ATA), founded in the United States in 1969, teaches its own Songahm forms. The Global Taekwondo Federation (GTF), founded in 1990 by a former ITF master, keeps Ko-Dang and adds patterns of its own. Many independent schools still teach the kwan-era forms under names such as tang soo do.

Two families of forms

So a student today usually learns one of two sets of forms. This site covers both.

World Taekwondo · poomsae 품새

  • Set by the Kukkiwon; used by World Taekwondo schools and in Olympic-style clubs
  • 8 Taegeuk forms for coloured belts, 9 black-belt forms
  • Front stance is ap kubi; counted by Kukkiwon move numbers
  • All 17 on this site

ITF · tul 틀

  • Devised by General Choi; used by ITF schools
  • 24 patterns from Chon-Ji to Tong-Il, each on its own diagram
  • Front stance is gunnun sogi; sine-wave motion
  • Planned on this site
