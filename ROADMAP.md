## Phase 1 · Site structure

### #5 · Title / landing page
**Goal:** a front door at `visualtkd.com` that explains the site and links to every section, in the same look as the form pages (Noto Serif KR headings, Plex body, the same tokens, light and dark).

**Plan**
1. Move the current forms index to `/forms/`, and put a new landing page at `/`. Printed QR codes only point at form pages and `/go/` links, so moving the index is safe; `/` keeps working.
2. Landing sections:

   - **Start here:** three cards: Forms (Taegeuk / black-belt), Technique library (phase 1, #6), How to read a diagram.
   - **About:** what the site is, who it's for, that it follows Kukkiwon, and how the print sheets and QR codes work.
   - **Progress:** forms published (the meter from the index).
   - **Footer:** sources policy and contact.
3. "How to read a diagram" becomes its own short page (`/how-to-read/`), pulling together the legend text now spread across the form pages' side notes.
4. Shared header and footer for all pages: a site nav (Forms · Library · How to read · About) generated by the build, so every page matches.

**Done when:** `/`, `/forms/`, `/how-to-read/` are live in the same style. Every form page has the shared nav, there is no horizontal scroll on phones, and the old `/` links still work.

### #6 · Details section (stances, blocks, strikes, kicks) and #2 · hand and foot details
These are one feature: a **technique library** that the form diagrams link into.

**Plan**
1. **One source of truth.** Move the technique and stance tables out of the template (`T`, `STANCE`) into `data/techniques.json`. Each entry gets:
   - slug, Korean, Hangul, English;
   - family (stance / block / strike / kick / hand / foot);
   - striking or blocking surface (links to #2);
   - target height;
   - a 2–3 sentence description in our own words;
   - the forms and move ids where it appears, built automatically by scanning `forms/*.json`.
   The template and the library pages both read this file.
2. **Pages.** One page per family with an anchor per technique, so links stay short and stable:
   `/library/stances/#ap-kubi`, `/library/blocks/#arae-makki`, `/library/strikes/`, `/library/kicks/`, and `/library/hands-feet/#sonnal` (#2).
   - Each entry shows the figure (front and side), the footprint glyph for stances, the surface used, key points, common mistakes, and "appears in" links to the exact moves.
3. **#2, hand and foot details.** The hands-and-feet page shows each striking or blocking surface (fist face, knife-hand edge, spear-hand tips, palm heel, arc hand, back fist; ball of the foot, foot blade, instep, heel). Every technique entry names its surface and links to it.
4. **Links from the form pages:**
   - the technique name and stance chip on every figure card, carousel slide and table row link to the library entry;
   - the study panel gets a "Technique details" link;
   - print sheets stay as they are (no extra QR codes).
5. **Scope today:** 13 stances and 60 techniques across the 11 built forms. Text and current figures first; the art is replaced in phase 3.

**Done when:** every technique used in the built forms has an entry, and every form page links to it. The library's own "appears in" lists are generated, and the build fails if a form uses a technique with no library entry.

**Needs from Brian:** confirm the URL scheme (`/library/<family>/#<slug>`). These paths may be printed later, so they should be settled once.

## Phase 2 · Diagram clarity

### #4 · Moves where the stance doesn't change
67 of the 352 moves in the built forms keep the same stance and change only the arms (block then punch, double punches, a strike after a kick lands). Today each one is a full new figure, so the reader has to spot what changed.

**Plan**
1. Research how printed manuals and kata books handle it: sequence frames, motion lines, ghosted previous position, numbered arm overlays. Collect the references in `design/same-stance.md`.
2. Prototype three treatments on Taegeuk Il Jang and one longer form (Pal Jang), and let Brian pick:
   - **A. Carry-over:** the legs are drawn lighter with a small "same stance" marker; only the moving arm is in full ink, with a short motion arc from where it was.
   - **B. Combined card:** one figure per stance, with the arm positions numbered on it (2-1, 2-2); the table and carousel still step through each count.
   - **C. Inset strip:** the full figure for the first count, and small arm-only thumbnails for the following counts.
   My recommendation is **A**: it keeps "one figure per count" (CLAUDE.md), the board stays the same size, and it reads clearly at card size.
3. Detection is automatic: same stance, lead foot and facing as the previous move, and not a kick. No data changes are needed; an optional `"carry": false` overrides it.
4. Update the legend, the carousel and the print sheet; record the rule in CLAUDE.md.

**Done when:** Brian has picked a treatment, it is applied to all 11 forms, and the print sheets still fit on 2 pages.

### #3 · Footwork diagram
Brian's example in the issue shows the right idea at low quality. *I couldn't open the attachment from this workspace, so the plan below follows the issue text.*

**Plan**
1. Research footwork notation from kata and dance books (step arrows, pivot arcs, start and end footprints), and put the references in `design/footwork.md`. Use Brian's example as the brief.
2. **Floor simulator (the main piece).** Compute where each foot actually is after every move from the data already in the forms: start position, stance, lead foot and facing. Each stance has a known length and width, and a turn pivots on a defined foot. Output per move: both feet's positions and angles before and after, which foot moved, and whether it was a step, slide, pivot or no movement.
   This also replaces the hand-placed minimap positions (`pos`), which REVIEW.md lists as approximate, and gives exact positions on the floor plan.
3. **Footwork diagram per move:**
   - top-down, the previous footprints in outline, the new ones filled (red = lead foot, as now);
   - an arrow along the path of the moving foot, a pivot arc for turns, and a small facing tick;
   - it replaces the static footprint glyph on cards, slides and the study panel.
4. A **whole-form footwork strip** on each form page (and optionally on print page 2): the full path drawn on the floor line, numbered.
5. Data overrides for moves the simulator can't infer (slides, jumps, Keumgang's 360° turns): an optional `"step"` field.

**Done when:** every move shows where the feet go, the minimap uses computed positions, and Brian has checked the diagrams on two forms against his example.

**Needs from Brian:** the example image from #3 committed to `design/refs/` (or a description). The simulator will also surface floor-position questions per form; those go in REVIEW.md.

## Phase 3 · Character v2 (after the MS-1–MS-8 review)

### #7 · New figure rig (new tracking issue)
Rebuild the renderer around the approved model sheets.

**Plan**
1. Brian reviews MS-1 to MS-8 in GitHub issues #9–#16 (sub-issues of #7) and logs corrections there; approved sheets are closed. Sheets marked "fix" are regenerated (Higgsfield, when re-enabled) until approved.
2. **New skeleton:**
   - hip and chest yaw separate from foot direction (dwit kubi turns the torso about 90°);
   - knee direction as its own control (out to the side in dwit kubi and juchum seogi);
   - rear leg locked straight in ap kubi;
   - feet always drawn last in each leg, so they're never hidden.
3. **New drawing**, traced from the approved sheets: realistic proportions, light cross-contour shading, no ears, new fists and open hands (from MS-8), and real foot shapes (side, front, back, top).
4. Pose library per stance (MS-1 to MS-6) and kick (MS-7), checked in front, side and back views against the sheets with `tools/check/figs.py`.
5. Performance and print checks: the figures still render quickly with 60 cards on a board, and still print clearly at 0.41 scale.
